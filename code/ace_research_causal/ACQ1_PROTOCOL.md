# ACE R5 — Active causal identification: locked public protocol (ACQ1)

Classification: **public exploratory synthetic research only**. This file is committed before the full 100-world-per-cohort ACQ1 evaluation. Initial design and mechanisms are researcher-authored and visible, not blinded. No OAIE/general-causal-intelligence claim.

## Question
After 80 randomized encouragement and 80 passive observational samples, does a bounded policy choosing whether to buy **direct** do(X) measurements improve **net** action utility beyond never buying, always buying, an uncertainty-only trigger, and a budget-matched random purchase control? Can a staged 8+24 query policy save evaluator cost without creating unacceptable false decisions?

## Worlds and access
Reuse the frozen definitions **only as source dependency** from `invalid_iv.py` on this branch; do not edit its implementation or original results. Five cohort labels and *new* seed bands: `valid` 710000–710099, `direct_exclusion` 720000–720099, `selected_compliance` 730000–730099, `both_invalid` 740000–740099, `weak_first_stage` 750000–750099. World prior and generator are exactly `invalid_iv.world(seed,cohort)`, with initial encouragement and observation samples generated by `invalid_iv.one`. Source parameters and true do-outcome are hidden from the policy.

Every algorithm begins with the **same** 80 encouragement (balanced Z = ±1) and 80 observational rows, from independent seeded generators. The baseline 160 acquisition rows are charged equally to all algorithms. Candidate direct `do(X)` samples (Z forcibly zero) are issued in balanced 8-row batches (4 at X=-1 and 4 at X=+1), from a separate deterministic random stream. An oracle ledger increments **only when a policy actually requests a direct sample**; any read beyond 32 or non-batch-of-8 request fails. For a given world, all policies requesting n do rows get the same prefix in the same order. Evaluation uses analytic true population utility and cannot feed it to the learner.

## Action utility and declared economic units
Action choices `do(X=-1)`, `do(X=+1)`, abstain; action cost = **0.50 utility unit**; direct sample price = **0.0125 utility unit per row**. Query budget = **32 rows**, so full batch costs 0.40 utility; first 8 rows cost 0.10. True expected action utility: `action * tau - 0.5` for action ∈{-1,+1} and 0 for abstention. An oracle with knowledge of tau chooses max(0, abs(tau)-0.5), defining regret. **Net regret = action regret + purchased_rows * 0.0125**. Never call the extra rows “free” or omit them from comparison.

## Learning and policies (frozen for primary evaluation)
Initial IV estimated via instrument reduced-form / first-stage ratio, OLS via passive X,Y regression, `invalid_iv.regress`. No known validity label provided. Point action rule: if |estimate| > 0.5, choose sign; otherwise abstain. Reject nonfinite estimates.

- `no_buy`: act from initial IV only, zero extra rows.
- `always32`: buy 32 do rows and fit direct-do OLS estimate; act from it.
- `uncertainty32`: buy 32 when | |IV|-0.5 | < 0.20 OR first-stage < 0.16, else no buy.
- `disagreement32` (primary candidate): buy 32 if IV/OLS signs conflict OR |IV - OLS| > 0.75 OR first-stage < 0.16. Otherwise use IV.
- `staged8_24`: same disagreement trigger, buy 8 do rows first. Buy remaining 24 if the 8-row action differs from IV action, OR | |direct8| - 0.5 | < 0.30. Use the most recently acquired direct estimate, even if this underperforms. No oracle peeking.
- `random_matched32`: random subset of exactly K worlds in each cohort, where K is the **precomputed count** of disagreement-triggered worlds (the latter uses only initial rows, no outcome truth). Purchases 32 rows/world; comparison matches cohort-level direct-label budget but is an **offline control** using cohort-level purchase count, not a deployable world-local policy.

All strategies share baseline 160 labels per world. Sampling and policy decisions deterministic under fixed seeds. Direct estimator uses no IV or observational outcome after acquisition, avoiding unreported tuning; no data-driven policy training across evaluation worlds.

## Metrics and falsifiers (must publish all)
For every cohort × policy: mean/total net regret, mean action regret, mean direct-label purchase cost, total direct rows, purchase rate, action acceptance rate, count wrong among accepted (conditional wrong rate is **null** when no accepted), and optimal-action rate. Report paired 2000-resample world bootstrap intervals on net-regret differences; treat descriptive only.

Before scoring the 100 worlds/cohort:
- F1: `disagreement32` improves net regret over `no_buy` by >= 0.10 in `direct_exclusion`. **If not, the targeted policy fails.**
- F2: `disagreement32` improves net regret over `random_matched32` by >= 0.02 in **both** `direct_exclusion` and `both_invalid`. **If not, its trigger adds no demonstrated targeting value.**
- F3: `disagreement32` increases net regret over `no_buy` by no more than 0.10 on `valid`; otherwise buying information does more harm than good on identifiable worlds.
- F4: `staged8_24` uses fewer direct rows than `disagreement32` and its net regret is not worse by more than 0.10 in `both_invalid`. Both must hold.
- F5: `uncertainty32` fails to protect against confident-wrong exclusion violations (report as a failure if mean net regret > 0.20 under `direct_exclusion`).
- F6: any universal “causal intelligence” or “safe acquisition” claim is **REJECTED** regardless of these outcomes because valid instrument identification is not testable in general from finite (Z,X,Y) without identifying assumptions.
