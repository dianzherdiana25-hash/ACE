# E1.4j — pre-score query synthesis protocol

**Parent** [E1.4i result](https://github.com/dianzherdiana25-hash/ACE/commit/9c469c22eb6a4b094d8819b8776d1c4344b89838) · [Issue #3](https://github.com/dianzherdiana25-hash/ACE/issues/3).

- Study two supplied grammars for exact words of length five and six, separately on S symbolic sequences and G graph chains. Allocate all natural inputs before drawing their motif. Six natural blocks per length/family, 24 worlds. Four engineered blocks per family/condition and two mirrored worlds per block, 48 worlds. Prior E1.4 public raw inputs are excluded from the bank. Motifs and event shapes may recur. Generated queries are limited to five or six events and never match probe length. The worker receives no probe, true word, hidden query label, condition, or mirror identifier.
- Each engineered condition gives both mirrors the same labeled demos and has a fixed pool with no disagreement between the two target words. In `single`, a shared positive and negative cover leave one short and one long word. In `sequence` and `beyond`, negative covers leave two and three short words respectively, and at least as many long words. The short candidates do not overlap as consecutive windows of a six-event trace. On the all-blank path, any legal query excludes at most one short and one long candidate. Thus a single query cannot guarantee separation in `sequence` and two cannot guarantee it in `beyond`. Querying the short words individually guarantees separation in one, two and three queries respectively within this language. Actual outcomes can resolve sooner if a positive answer eliminates one class.
- Compare three policies using the same fit and ranking. Guided synthesizes up to eight candidates per round by interleaving seeded samples from the compatible short and long words and rendering their event strings. Random samples events without using the fitted words and may draw up to 64 event strings to obtain eight unique, unlabeled proposals. Fixed ranks the eight bank inputs without generating replacements. All policies skip already labeled event strings and evaluate at most eight distinct inputs per round. Compare the same adaptive policy prefixes under caps of zero, one and two purchased labels. Record actual inputs ranked, proposal attempts, fit comparisons, model checks, CPU/memory and label counts separately. This matches caps for scored inputs and labels, with different work for proposal construction.
- For each offered question, predict the possible labels from both exact-fit classes. For each possible answer compute the smaller surviving fraction across classes and minimize the worst such fraction. Break ties by shorter question, then greater mass of class-exclusive answers under an equal mixture of the two uniform classes, then frozen proposal order. Buy no label if no input reduces the worst surviving fraction. State a length only when one class remains fully consistent. Abstain otherwise and use the best short word as a prediction fallback. Choose the lexicographically first best-fitting word within the diagnosed or fallback class. The oracle reveals only the chosen label. Record correct diagnosis, wrong diagnosis, abstention, exact probes and false-positive probes independently.
- **SYNTHESIS_DEVELOPMENT_GO** requires in each family, at cap one, both mirrors correctly diagnosed in at least 3/4 `single` blocks and guided aggregate diagnoses exceeding each control. In `sequence`, at least 3/4 long worlds must resolve only on the second guided label, and guided cap-two correct diagnoses must exceed each control. In `beyond`, at least 3/4 long worlds must remain abstained under cap two. Any wrong guided diagnosis or failed threshold yields **SYNTHESIS_HOLD**. Natural results are descriptive, and **NATURAL_HOLD** for transfer carries forward.
- Freeze bank and sources before scoring and bind the upstream freeze manifests. Run trusted workers under native Bubblewrap with separate evaluator state, a resumable ledger, explicit deadlines, a fresh replay ledger, and arithmetic audit. This tests generation of experiments between existing representations in a restricted language.
