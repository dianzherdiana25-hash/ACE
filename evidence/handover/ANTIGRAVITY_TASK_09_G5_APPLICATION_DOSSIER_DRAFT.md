## 1. Purpose

Design the first live application of the G5 clauses:

- **C1** — for each assessed unit, a summary exists carrying
  all four FR-09 fields in Indonesian, in plain language:
  **DICOBA** (what was tried) · **HASIL** (the result) ·
  **KEYAKINAN** (confidence) · **BATAS** (limits of
  interpretation). A missing field is a named failure.
- **C2** — the summary reached the owner through a channel
  they read on their phone, and understanding its result and
  the resulting decision requires opening no code, PR, or
  technical document.
- **C3** — comprehension evidenced by **recorded owner use**:
  the owner acting on summaries alone — a decision, direction,
  or correction whose content references the summary's claims.

Outcome vocabulary, exactly: `ACCESS_DEMONSTRATED` /
`DELIVERY_ONLY` / `NOT_DEMONSTRATED` (missing items named).

## 2. The governing fence: harvest, never stage

G5's evidence species is unlike every other gate's. G1–G4
measure artifacts and runs the program creates on purpose.
G5 measures whether a *person* — the owner — can and does use
what the program reports. The coordinator's fence, adopted
here as a design axiom:

- The evidence protocol **harvests** owner use that occurred
  naturally and is already recorded in the program's own
  records. It **never stages** anything for the owner: no
  comprehension quiz, no test summary written to elicit a
  response, no task, and no approval request manufactured to
  produce evidence.
- Under the Emancipation amendment the owner is the program's
  **observer**, receiving reports — not a lab subject, and
  not an approval queue. A protocol that burdened the owner
  to manufacture its own evidence would falsify the very
  accessibility it claims to measure.
- Corollary: the assessment is **retrospective by
  construction**. The period (§3) is fixed by rule, the record
  already exists, and nothing in the assessed stream may be
  edited, re-sent, or supplemented after the period is fixed.
  If the harvest finds the record thin, the outcome is
  `DELIVERY_ONLY` or `NOT_DEMONSTRATED` — published as
  measured, never repaired by prompting the owner.

## 3. Assessed units and period

- **Unit:** one owner-facing consolidated summary delivered
  in the discussion room that reports a stage result, a
  measured finding, or a program decision (the reports ila
  consolidates from the benches' work). Pure conversation,
  greetings, and raw bench reports in other rooms are not
  units; the consolidated discussion-room stream is the
  delivery channel C2 names ("in current practice: the
  discussion-room reports").
- **Enumeration rule (mechanical, anti-cherry-picking):**
  every message in the discussion room within the period
  that reports a stage result or program decision is a unit;
  the harvester lists *all* of them with message references,
  including units expected to score badly. The denominator is
  published in the dossier.
- **Period:** from the Emancipation amendment (2026-11-12
  evening — the point at which owner-facing reporting became
  the program's governance channel) to a cutoff fixed in the
  preregistration. The window is chosen by this rule, not by
  inspecting which window flatters the score (§10, A3).

## 4. C1 protocol — the FR-09 completeness rubric

Each unit is scored field by field against these anchors. A
field is present only if a reader who reads *nothing else*
can extract it from the summary itself:

- **DICOBA** — names the work attempted (a stage, an
  experiment, a build), not merely its outcome.
- **HASIL** — states the measured result: the outcome word
  and its load-bearing numbers (counts, verdicts, amounts),
  not a promise or a vibe.
- **KEYAKINAN** — states how far the result can be trusted:
  what was verified, by whom/how, and what remains unverified
  (e.g. "terverifikasi dari cakram", "belum terverifikasi",
  evidence-vs-claim markers).
- **BATAS** — states what the result does *not* show: scope
  limits, caveats, what would change the conclusion.
- **Language & plainness:** the field-carrying text is
  Indonesian; a field whose content exists only as a code
  identifier, PR number, digest, or file path does not count
  as present (that is precisely the "technical detour" C2
  excludes, one level down).

Scoring discipline: two independent scorers apply the rubric;
disagreements are recorded per unit per field and resolved by
the rubric text, not by negotiation toward a verdict. A unit
is **complete** at 4/4 fields.

**Period criterion (fixed here, before assessment):** C1
passes iff **≥90% of units are complete** *and* **every unit
carries HASIL** (a stream that sometimes omits results
entirely is not summarising). The stricter alternative —
100% completeness — is named as decision D-G5-2 for the
coordinator; the design text itself scores a missing field
as a named failure *per unit*, which this criterion preserves
in the dossier even when the period passes.

## 5. C2 protocol — delivery without a technical detour

Per unit, both parts must hold:

1. **Channel:** the unit was delivered in the discussion-room
   stream (message reference + timestamp in the dossier) —
   the channel the owner reads on his phone.
2. **Self-containment:** the unit's result and any decision
   it carries can be understood from the unit alone. A unit
   that defers its substance elsewhere ("detailnya di PR
   #…", "lihat addendum") fails this part even if a link was
   tapped later; supplementary links for the curious are
   fine, load-bearing ones are not.

**Period criterion:** C2 passes iff ≥90% of units satisfy
both parts (same threshold family as C1, for coherence).

## 6. C3 protocol — harvesting recorded owner use

**Definition — a qualifying USE EVENT** is a recorded owner
message (in any room, or relayed verbatim into program
records) meeting all three tests:

1. **Sequence:** it follows a delivered summary (unit) in
   time.
2. **Reference:** its content references that summary's
   claims — a result, number, finding, or course of action
   the summary reported (quoted, paraphrased, or named
   unambiguously).
3. **Action:** it *does* something with those claims —
   issues a decision, a direction, a correction, or a
   resource commitment whose content depends on them.

**Exclusions (named in advance):** bare acknowledgements
("ok", "lanjut") with no content reference · owner-originated
initiatives with no antecedent summary (the owner's own ideas
are his, not evidence about summaries) · bare approvals of
options the program presented (an approval-queue reflex; an
approval that *adds content* — amends, conditions, corrects —
is an action under test 3 and counts).

**Harvest method:** the harvester first enumerates *all*
owner decision/direction/correction messages in the period
from the program's records (checkpoint addenda and the
decisions log cite them), then scores each against tests
1–3 with the owner's words quoted in the dossier. The full
candidate list — including candidates that fail — is part of
the dossier, so the denominator cannot be curated silently
(§10, A2).

**Candidate events already visible in the record** (listed
for the harvester to score — *not* pre-judged here): the
Rp0 direction amendment ("Rp0 adalah disiplin awal, bukan
sumpah") · the Emancipation amendment itself · the
room-network operating model and the "operations room is a
work post, not a reporting surface" correction · the Lynk.id
login-mechanism correction · the KBM support-contact
strike-off · the local-brain succession decision (replace
the director, download approved, 8B to be museum-ised).

**Period criterion (fixed here, before assessment):** C3
passes iff **≥3 qualifying use events**, spanning **≥2
distinct program domains** (governance, resources/funding,
operations, venues/business, technical direction), including
**≥1 correction** — an owner act that overrules or amends a
reported course. A correction is the strongest comprehension
signal there is: one cannot correct what one has not
understood.

## 7. Outcome mapping

| C1 | C2 | C3 | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| pass | pass | pass | `ACCESS_DEMONSTRATED` |
| pass | pass | fail | `DELIVERY_ONLY` |
| fail | any | any | `NOT_DEMONSTRATED`, units/fields named |
| pass | fail | any | `NOT_DEMONSTRATED`, units named |

A C3 failure is recorded as a property of **ACE's reporting
system** (use was not evidenced), never as a failing of the
owner (§10, A6).

## 8. Today's status — as it stands, not a score

- **C2's channel exists and is daily practice:** consolidated
  Indonesian reports in the discussion room are how the owner
  in fact follows the program from his phone.
- **C3 candidate evidence is plentiful and recorded** (§6
  list) — this is the rare gate whose hardest clause may
  already have its evidence lying in the record.
- **C1 is the weak clause.** The report stream is narrative,
  written to inform, not against the FR-09 rubric: HASIL is
  reliably strong and DICOBA usually present, but KEYAKINAN
  and BATAS are uneven — explicit in some streams (the bench
  reports mark verified/unverified as a matter of discipline),
  implicit or absent in others. Scored strictly today, the
  plausible landing zone is `DELIVERY_ONLY` or a narrow
  `ACCESS_DEMONSTRATED` decided by the C1 threshold — this
  draft deliberately does not call it; making that call
  decidable is the dossier's whole job.

## 9. Prerequisites

- **P-G5-1 — the G5 instrument exists.** None has been built
  (PR #136 specifies G4 and G5 scorers; neither exists). Build
  per the design's implementation note: pure scorer over the
  harvested dossier files, per-clause named failures, criteria
  version; adversarial synthetic tests per the design
  (summary missing the confidence field; delivery with no
  recorded use; a use event that fails the reference test).
- **P-G5-2 — the harvest is executed and sealed.** A named
  harvester produces the dossier: unit list (§3), per-unit
  rubric sheets (§4–§5), use-event candidate list with quotes
  (§6). Two-hats discipline: ila authors most of the assessed
  summaries, so ila does not harvest alone — harvest by Ian
  or the operations post, verification by this bench, or an
  equivalent split recorded in the preregistration.
- **P-G5-3 — period and thresholds ratified** (§11) before
  the harvest begins, so no threshold is fitted to the
  record it will judge.

## 10. Risks and ambiguities (named, not resolved)

- **A1 — Rubric softness.** Field presence is a judgment,
  not a hash. The anchors (§4) and double scoring narrow it;
  the residual subjectivity is recorded, with per-field
  disagreement counts published in the dossier. A future
  template (§10 A4) shrinks this risk prospectively; it
  cannot be removed retrospectively.
- **A2 — Harvest selection bias.** Mitigated structurally:
  enumerate-then-score (§6), full candidate list published.
  The bias that remains is in the program's records
  themselves (acts that were never written down cannot be
  harvested) — named here so a thin harvest is read as a
  record property first.
- **A3 — Window gaming.** The period rule (§3) is fixed to a
  governance milestone, not to outcomes; any future period
  extension is by the same rule (milestone → cutoff), never
  by choosing dates around known events.
- **A4 — The template question.** Adopting an explicit FR-09
  template for future consolidated reports would make future
  C1 scoring nearly mechanical — a legitimate improvement to
  the *reporting system* (which is what G5 measures). It must
  never be back-applied: a period straddling the adoption is
  scored as the record stands, with the adoption date
  disclosed in the dossier.
- **A5 — Multi-room attribution.** The owner reads several
  rooms; a use event typed in one room may respond to a
  summary in another. Test 2 (reference) governs: the event
  must reference content that appeared in an assessed unit,
  wherever the response was typed. Content the owner could
  only have gotten from raw bench streams is not C3 evidence
  for the consolidated stream.
- **A6 — Silence is not failure.** Periods where the owner
  simply watches produce no use events; C3 then fails as
  *not evidenced* and the outcome is `DELIVERY_ONLY`. The
  gate measures whether ACE's reporting demonstrably lands —
  it does not grade the owner's attention, and the dossier
  language must keep that distinction visible to any reader.
